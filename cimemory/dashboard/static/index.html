The Staircase

A standard is never lowered and never deleted. Each standardised countermeasure raises the baseline one step and resets it — that is the ratchet. The dashed line is the identical agent without memory: it drifts the other way.

Every step down is one cycle: plan five times over, do once, check on results and process, act by folding the countermeasure into the standard.

A rejected countermeasure leaves the staircase flat — the baseline does not move until the gain is proven to hold.

A kaikaku is the only jump: the roots change, not the process.
